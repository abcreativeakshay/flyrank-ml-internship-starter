## The keepers (all real captures of my own working dashboards)

1. `keeper_dashboard_overview.png` — the combined DMDW dashboard overview. Real data, real interface, shows the warehouse numbers a visitor can verify.
2. `keeper_jury3_3d_clusters.jpg` — the Jury 3 app's 3D cluster view of the stroke-risk segmentation (Ward clustering, k=4). The single most distinctive visual from the project.
3. `keeper_forecast_pattern_matching.jpg` — Jury 2 forecast app, pattern-matching view on the no-show dataset.
4. `keeper_noshow_pattern_matching.jpg` — Jury 2 no-show analysis view. Kept alongside #3 because the pair shows the same method on two different problems.

## What I rejected, and why

- **Near-duplicate dashboard views** — several screenshots show the same pages with one filter changed. One strong view per page tells the story; duplicates make a portfolio look padded.
- **Generated diagram graphics** (e.g. the K-Means study-sheet art) — they look polished but they are not my work or my data. On a page whose claim is "systems that ship and get measured", a decorative AI graphic argues against me.
- **Any screenshot containing other people's personal data** — kept out on principle, the same rule I applied to the COGNIVA admin dashboard (real users' names, emails and phone numbers never go in a public portfolio).

## Where I chose a real capture over AI

Everywhere the subject is the work itself. The dashboards above are screenshots of running apps with real numbers; an AI-generated "dashboard-style" image would be fiction. AI helped me select, crop and order the set — it did not create any of the images.
